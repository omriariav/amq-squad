# amq-squad 3.1 plan, revision 4 (2026-10-30)

Built with amq-squad 3.0.0 itself. The plan of record for 3.0 is
docs/v3-plan.md; its rules (R1 to R52, the amendments, the briefs rulings)
stay in force unless a line here changes one, and every change names the
line it changes. Follow-ups carried into 3.1 are in docs/v3-followups.md.

## 1. Goal, in the owner's words

"We want to copy claude agents team but with more binaries and models, and
experience needs to be seamless. The other main objective is to be able to
work remotely via buzz or grok bot." (2026-10-30)

The interaction model to copy, named by the owner from OpenMuse's README and
taken as a reference only, no dependency: ask for an outcome, follow the
plan, review actions, come back to the result.

## 2. The user story 3.1 ships

1. One binary. In a folder, `amq-squad lead` is a conversation with the
   lead on pi.
2. The owner says what they want. The lead decides whether it works alone
   or needs a team.
3. If it needs a team, its reply is a plan the owner can read: what it will
   do alone, which seats it opens with which binary and model, what each
   seat touches, what the owner will be asked to approve. The plan carries
   the squad.yaml digest. The owner's "yes" is the digest confirmation;
   git init and `amq coop init` run underneath; the team starts.
4. The owner watches and steers from the same conversation, on the
   owner's turn: the lead is turn-based, so on each owner turn it reads
   the seats' mail and status (amq_drain, amq_status) and reports; nothing
   drives it between turns (an event-driven lead turn is not 3.1).
5. The typed actions (tag, release, external send, credential change,
   growth beyond the grant) still stop for the owner's signature over Buzz.
6. The same conversation works from the phone, both ways: the owner
   writes, the lead answers there, progress and results arrive there.

What 3.1 does not do: a Grok bot face (3.2, a second face on the same gate
transport after the signing question is settled); Linux seats (the 3.1
candidate list in follow-ups, not this revision); OpenMuse's engine,
browser container or app.

## 3. Design rulings (reviewer, 2026-10-30), with the lines they change

### 3.1 One lead: the solo lead gains squad tools

- The solo lead gains the tools to propose and start a squad. The operator
  session gains no chat face. The mayor stays what R40 makes it: the fleet
  policy point in the start path (grant, max squads, cost cap fail-closed,
  permitted repositories), called as a library by the lead's start, never
  a second conversational actor.
- Why: R40's "the mayor never answers a gate" is protected by keeping the
  chat partner and the gate path in different processes. The one lead may
  raise a gate and report its state; its tool set never carries a
  gate-answer verb. The seam the owner hit ("I'm solo here") is the solo
  lead's, so the fix belongs where the conversation is.
- **New rule R53 (phase as a tool-list fact).** The one lead has two
  phases: solo (it may edit; it is the owner's single agent) and team
  (after the owner's yes). 3.0 already gives `lead --team` a narrowed tool
  list, TeamLeadTools (internal/piruntime/launch.go: amq_send, amq_drain,
  amq_reply, amq_task_list, amq_task_add, amq_task_reset, amq_repo_read,
  amq_repo_list, amq_status; no edit, write or shell tool; 3.0.1 t16 adds
  amq_task_widen and amq_scheduler_relaunch, the lead's two decisions over
  the scheduler's socket, each running its CLI verb from the lead's own
  process); what does not
  exist is the switch inside one conversation. R53: on the owner's yes the
  lead's tool list becomes TeamLeadTools, the existing set, for the rest
  of the conversation; the solo set goes and the team set comes (removing
  edit and write alone is not enough, since a shell tool edits through
  sed). A prompt sentence is not the mechanism; a local model will not
  hold one across a two-minute turn.
- The owner's yes is the digest confirmation as today: the plan the lead
  prints carries the squad.yaml digest; the yes runs `team apply` with that
  digest. For a root outside the fleet grant, R40's fallback holds: the
  lead proposes, the owner starts. For the dogfood the repository is in
  the grant.
- **Two faces of one lead (reviewer, 2026-10-30; the owner's order).** The
  squad tools are built once as CLI verbs: `team propose --from` (the plan
  with the digest), `team apply` with the digest, the start through the
  mayor's library path, git init and coop init underneath. The pi face
  wraps them as native tools; the Claude face is the orchestrator skill
  plus the verbs in the seat's shell under guard. The owner's order: the
  manager on pi with a local model first (the plan, dispatch, read and
  relay job is small; a smaller, faster local entry than the solo default
  may be named for the manager role), strong Claude and Codex seats for
  the work; the Claude lead seat is the designed fallback, on the
  subscription login with no API key, chosen by the measured run 1b. pi
  with an Anthropic or OpenAI key stays available as a strong lead the day
  a key exists, since the verbs are shared. A Codex lead is not 3.1:
  codex-cli has no hook point to enforce the phase switch inside a
  conversation (its sandbox is per launch); a Codex lead can be 3.2 by a
  restart into a read-only sandbox at team start.
- **The lead seat leads an existing team; it cannot open the one it
  belongs to.** A lead seat exists only after team apply and start-squad
  ran, so "the lead opens the team" is always the pi manager's job (or the
  owner's, by team apply and start-squad as today). The fallback is three
  things: (a) acceptance row 1c, a Claude lead seat started by
  start-squad takes the task list and leads a brief to the train, the
  owner talking to it in its pane or by mail; (b) a supervisor with no
  model, `lead --supervise --seats FILE --lead HANDLE`, the team-mode
  supervision path (ruling 15) minus the model loop, started by
  start-squad, its pid in the launch record, exiting with the scheduler,
  the only caller of --lead-pool for a seat lead; (c) R53 for the seat is
  the allow list at launch: dontAsk, `Bash(amq-squad *)` and the read-only
  tools, no Edit, Write or NotebookEdit, no gate verb, the hooks guard as
  the second fence. Neither the lead seat nor the supervisor answers a
  gate; the lead's allow list has no gate-answer verb and the guard
  refuses it, as for seats today (R40 unchanged).
- **R53 on a Claude Code conversation (the PreToolUse twin).** Its real
  case is the orchestrator skill in the owner's own Claude Code session
  that opens a team and switches phase: the team start writes a phase
  marker in the session dir, and the PreToolUse hook denies Edit, Write,
  NotebookEdit and Bash while the marker says team, allowing only the
  amq-squad verbs the skill names. Harness-enforced, so it meets R53's
  standard. Not the lead seat's mechanism (that is the allow list above);
  the two Claude faces are distinct.
- **New rule R55 (the confirmation record; R40 in verb form).** `team apply
  --digest D` succeeds only if a confirmation record for D exists, younger
  than 30 minutes (the gate expiry), written by a process the model does
  not control, through one of three doors: the owner's own shell,
  `amq-squad team confirm D` (the guard refuses it from any seat); a
  signed DM `yes <digest prefix>` through the mailbox handler's typed path;
  or the pi TUI, where the runtime's input path, not the model, writes the
  record when the owner's typed line matches the printed digest. Else apply
  refuses and names the three doors. It holds for the manager and for the
  fallback alike. Acceptance twin on rows 1a and 1b: an apply with no
  record refuses; a record written from a seat's shell is refused by the
  guard.

### 3.2 Two-way phone: the core relays the lead's replies

- The fact: the mailbox adapter's reply_timeout is 60 s and a lead turn on
  the local model took about two minutes, so the lead's answer can never be
  the inbound's one non-status reply. Two-way needs an outbound post
  independent of any inbound.
- Ruling: the lead never holds a key. The lead's answers, progress and
  results are AMQ replies on the cockpit thread from the attach handle; the
  core (the attachment, which holds the body key for the question post
  under ruling 20 point 1 and R33) relays them to the DM.
- **R33 widened by one purpose, "lead reply relay"**: text only, through
  redact.Block, rate-bounded (one progress post per state change or per N
  minutes; every answer and every result), the key in the core's process
  only. The relay refuses to carry anything shaped like a gate answer or a
  typed approval; gate lines come only from the owner's signed DM through
  gateverify (R40). Inbound stays as today: the handler's one non-status
  reply becomes `accepted <request id>` at once; the answer arrives later
  as a relay post.
- The purpose retires with the body key when amq-remote offers a post
  surface on serve's connection. Upstream ask 19, filed at the start of
  brief 2 on the owner's go: "conversation mode on the mailbox adapter:
  every bodied reply on the thread posts until a final reply or the
  timeout".
- The chat partner is the lead (3.1), not the mayor.
- Owner decision 1 (pending): A, widen R33 now and retire later (two-way in
  3.1); B, wait for the upstream surface: under B the phone gets the
  receipt (`accepted` or `routed`) and nothing more, since serve posts only
  the newest non-status reply inside reply_timeout (60 s) and an answer or
  a result arrives minutes later; the owner gets answers by asking again
  on the next DM. Recommended: A.

### 3.4 The lead's proof for decisions (amendment to R4, 3.0.1 t16)

- R4 (2b-2 milestone B: the lead's decisions over the scheduler's own
  socket, accepted only from the lead's proved process) now has two forms,
  and one scheduler uses exactly one:
  - a `lead --team` start: the lead is the team-mode process that started
    this scheduler. Its pid and raw start stamp are recorded at the
    scheduler's start; a decision is accepted from a sender with an
    ancestor whose pid and stamp both equal them (the bounded parent-chain
    walk, the stamp re-read at every hop; a chain that reaches launchd
    without the match is origin_unknown). If the recorded incarnation is
    gone or its pid reused, the decision is refused with lead_gone and the
    scheduler exits rather than serve a dead lead's socket.
  - every other start: the lead is the AMQ-proved seat under the session
    root (the launch ledger's proof), unchanged. Brief 1's Claude lead
    seat (row 1c) is this form, so its fallback is untouched.
- Why: the roster's tmux session holds seats only. A lead inside it keeps
  AMQ's binding present and every roster relaunch fails by construction;
  a lead outside it has no ledger proof. The kernel's parent link is the
  team-mode lead's proof, and the stamp makes it sound: the recorded pid
  can appear in another chain only after the lead died and the pid was
  reused, and then it carries another stamp.
- The `--lead` seat is never in the roster, so neither form's lead is
  launched or relaunched into the roster's session.

naming the first lead's pid and pane. Today it is not refused: the second
scheduler's `ErrRunning` (the `run.lock` flock,
internal/schedproc/process.go:96-105) is a plain error mapped to

leaves its scheduler alive, since `piExit` calls `os.Exit`
(internal/cli/pi.go:277-278) and skips the deferred stop (pi_lead.go:122),
so the scheduler lives until its next lead_gone check (process.go:203-207).

`up` follows the same rule. If the recorded lead's pid is alive and its
pane exists, `up` refuses `lead_running`, prints the pid, the pane and the
attach command, and stops nothing. It stops only a lead whose pid is dead
or whose pane is gone, and a scheduler whose lead_gone check has passed,
and records what it stopped as a decision. It never kills a live lead.
