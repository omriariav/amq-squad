## The fact, restated
The mailbox adapter's reply_timeout is 60 s; a lead turn takes longer; so the inbound's one non-status reply can never carry the lead's answer. Inbound today: the handler's reply is `accepted <request id>` and the question goes to the lead's cockpit thread (internal/buzz/inbound.go, thread cockpit/<channel>). The owner never sees the answer unless he asks again. Two-way needs an outbound post independent of any inbound.

## The ruling, restated (do not re-decide)
- The lead never holds a key. The lead's answers, progress and results are AMQ replies on the cockpit thread from the lead's handle; the core (the attachment process, which holds the body key for the question post under ruling 20 point 1) relays them to the owner's DM.
- R33 gains exactly one purpose, "lead reply relay": text only, through redact.Block, rate-bounded, the key in the core's process only.
- The relay refuses to carry anything shaped like a gate answer or a typed approval: it runs gateverify's inbound parser over every outbound line and refuses a match. Gate lines come only from the owner's signed DM through gateverify (R40). The relay never posts a gate question either; those keep their own path.
- The purpose retires when amq-remote offers a post surface on serve's connection (upstream ask 19).

## The two numbers, fixed here
1. **Progress rate bound**: counted on the lead's progress-labeled replies: never more than one progress post per 2 minutes per cockpit thread (the newest label wins when several arrive inside the bound); every answer and every result post without a bound but at most one post per cockpit reply (no fan-out). A row fails when a burst of five progress-labeled replies in a minute yields more than one progress post.
2. **Gate-shaped refusal**, narrowed to the risk (the signature already makes a relay post unverifiable as the owner's answer; the refusal is for the owner's eyes and must not eat the lead's sentences): (a) the relay never posts into the thread of a gate question, only into the DM conversation it opened for the request, with a row; (b) a line is refused as an answer form only when the whole line is one (yes, no, approve, deny, with or without a gate id or the typed tuple, after trimming punctuation and case), never when it merely contains the word ("No, the build is green" posts); (c) an open gate's id (`rel-`/`gate-`/`<kind>-<20 hex>` from the open gate store) or an open gate's exact question text anywhere in the line refuses. The refusal is recorded as `relay_refused_gate_shape` with the line's sha256 only, never the line. A row per form, and a row that the sentence containing the word posts.

## The change
1. **The outbound relay in the attachment** (internal/buzz, the attach command's loop in internal/cli): the attachment already polls the owner's inbox; it now also follows each cockpit thread it opened for an inbound question (the thread id is in the question post it made) and relays new replies from the lead's handle: an answer (the reply to the question), progress (a reply the lead labels progress on its send; never a sentence the attachment composes from status: the relay carries only what the lead said, and the core is never a second author on the owner's phone), and the result (the reply that closes the request). Each relayed post carries the request id as its first line (`re <request id>`) so the owner can tell which question it answers; the text passes redact.Block; a post longer than Buzz's DM limit is split at paragraph boundaries with `(1/3)` marks, at most three parts, the rest replaced by one line naming the cockpit thread.
2. **Inbound, now a conversation**: a DM that is a reply to a relayed post continues that request's cockpit thread as a new lead turn (the relay record maps each post it made to its request and thread); the relayed answer to a continuation carries the same `re <request id>`; a DM that is not a reply opens a new request as today. `accepted <request id>` at once in both cases; a repeated question re-posts the current cockpit state as today. No change to gateverify's inbound path; a reply to a relayed post that is itself a whole-line answer form is not a gate answer (it reaches the lead as text) and the gate path never sees it unless it is a reply in a gate question's thread, as today.
3. **The lead side**: the lead's `/squad` screen and `status` show, per request id, whether the answer was relayed (`relayed_at`) or refused and why (name only). No new lead tool: the lead replies on the cockpit thread as it does today; the relay is the core's.
4. **Attach restart**: a relay record per request (`<root>/extensions/amq-squad/relay/<request id>.json`: cockpit thread, last relayed reply id, posts made with their sha256, refusals) so a restarted attachment neither re-posts nor skips; one attach per mailbox as today (a second attach refuses as today).
5. **Docs**: README's phone section (ask a question from Buzz, get `accepted`, the answer arrives as a post starting with `re <id>`; what the relay never carries); docs/status-contract.md for the relay fields; the execution note docs/v3-spike/31-evidence/31-4-two-way-phone.md with the design, the two numbers, the rows, the owner-run row (a question from the phone to the lead on this repository; the `accepted` receipt; the answer as a relay post; a progress post on a state change; a gate-shaped line typed by the lead refused and named in status), the retirement condition, and a line for the 3.1 release notes; the upstream ask 19 text as filed (file it only if the owner says go; otherwise the note carries the text and says it is unfiled).
